Meta Tags Guide: Write Titles & Descriptions That Get Clicks

Meta tags are the short pieces of text that tell Google — and people scrolling search results — what a page is about. You do not see them on the page itself, but you see their effect every time you search: the blue clickable title and the short text under it come from your title tag and meta description.

1. The title tag: keep it under 60 characters

A good title says what the page offers, includes the main topic once, and reads like something a person would click — not a list of keywords. If it runs much past 60 characters, Google usually cuts it off with “…”, so the most important words should come first.

Good example (52 characters): “Free Budget Planner: Plan Your Month in 5 Minutes”
Weak example: “Budget, money, finance, planner, calculator, tips, best, free, 2026, guide” — this tells nobody what they will actually get.

Check yours before publishing with the Title Length Checker.

2. The meta description: 120–155 characters, benefit first

The description does not directly raise rankings, but a clear, benefit-driven description earns more clicks — and more clicks are the point. Start with what the reader gets, add the topic once, and end with a small action.

Good example: “Plan your monthly budget in 5 minutes with a free planner — see needs, wants and savings at a glance, no signup needed.”
Weak example: “Welcome to our website. Click here for more information about many topics.”

Check the length with the Meta Description Length Checker.

3. Open Graph tags: control how your page looks when shared

When someone shares a page on Facebook, X or WhatsApp, Open Graph tags decide the preview title, description and image. Without them, the platform guesses — and the guess is often ugly. Set an og:title, og:description and og:image for every page you expect people to share.

5. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using the same title and description on every page — each page should be unique.
  • Stuffing the same keyword five times — it reads badly and helps nobody.
  • Writing a description that only says “welcome” instead of the benefit.
  • Forgetting Open Graph tags, so shared links show a blank or wrong preview.
  • Leaving the title or description blank and letting Google guess them for you.
